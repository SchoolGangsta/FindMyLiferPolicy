Privacy Policy — FindMyLifer

Last updated: 2026-09-18

FindMyLifer ("the app") helps birders find recent sightings of species they have not yet seen. We are committed to protecting your privacy. This policy explains what data the app uses, how it is used, and what is and is not transmitted off your device.

Information the app uses

Location

The app requests access to your device location while in use. To retrieve nearby bird sightings and hotspots, the coordinates of your search origin — your current location, or a place you picked on the map — are sent to our own lightweight backend proxy, which forwards the request to the eBird API (operated by the Cornell Lab of Ornithology) using our server-side API credentials. For country-wide searches, the country code of that origin is sent instead of coordinates. The proxy exists solely to keep those credentials off your device — it is not a database and does not require an account.

  • Before forwarding a nearby search, the proxy rounds the coordinates to two decimal places (roughly 1 km), so eBird receives an approximate search area rather than your exact position.
  • The proxy never writes your coordinates, or the hotspots you looked up, to its logs, and it keeps no record of who searched where.
  • To limit the load on eBird, the proxy keeps eBird's responses in memory for a short time — 5 minutes for sightings, up to 6 hours for hotspot lists, longer for reference data such as the species list — filed under the rounded search area, or the hotspot or country requested, so that people asking for the same thing share one copy. These cached responses contain only public eBird data and are not associated with you, your device, or your IP address.
  • Like any internet service, the proxy sees your device's IP address while it handles a request. We do not use it to identify you or to build a history of your searches.
  • We do not link your location to your identity.
  • We do not track your location in the background.
  • We do not share your location with advertising networks or analytics providers.

The eBird API receives the rounded latitude and longitude as part of standard search queries (via our proxy, not directly from your device — so eBird sees our proxy's IP address, not yours). eBird's Terms of Use govern their handling of that data.

Maps and place search

The app uses Apple's MapKit and location services to draw maps, to turn coordinates into a place name (for example, the town shown for your search area), and to find a place when you choose a different search origin. Those requests — coordinates, or the text you type into place search — go to Apple and are handled under Apple's Privacy Policy. We do not receive them.

Life list (CSV import)

If you choose to import your eBird life list, the file is parsed on your device and the species codes and first-seen dates are stored locally in iOS UserDefaults. The CSV is not uploaded anywhere.

iCloud sync. If you are signed in to iCloud, your life list (species codes and first-seen dates) is automatically mirrored to your private iCloud Key-Value storage so it survives reinstalling the app and syncs between your iPhones. It lives in your own iCloud account under Apple's terms; it is never sent to our proxy, and we have no access to it. Signing out of iCloud stops syncing; the list stays on the device.

Bird images

Photos shown in the app are fetched from the public iNaturalist API (operated by the California Academy of Sciences and National Geographic Society), filtered to species-page and community-submitted photos under open Creative Commons licenses (CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC BY-NC, CC BY-NC-SA); the photographer's credit is shown on the species detail screen. When no suitably licensed photo exists for a species, the app shows a generic bird photo loaded from Unsplash. Standard HTTP request metadata (IP address, user agent) is visible to iNaturalist, and to Unsplash when that fallback photo is shown, as part of those requests. No identifying information is sent.

Directions and links you open

When you ask for directions to a sighting, the app opens Apple Maps, Google Maps or Waze — whichever you pick — with the coordinates of the sighting, not your own location. "View on eBird" and checklist links open ebird.org. What those apps and sites do with your visit is governed by their own privacy policies.

Information we do NOT collect

  • We do not run any analytics or telemetry today.
  • We do not show ads.
  • We do not collect device identifiers, contacts, photos, or health data.
  • We do not have user accounts or sign-in.
  • We do not sell your data, and we never will — there is nothing to sell, since we don't collect anything beyond what's described above.

Your data, your control

  • Location permission can be granted, changed, or revoked anytime in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → FindMyLifer.
  • Deleting the app removes all data stored on your device (your imported life list, cached taxonomy, preferences). If iCloud sync was active, the copy of your life list in your own iCloud account remains, and is restored if you reinstall the app. To remove that copy too, use Settings → Manage Life List → Clear Entire Life List in the app before deleting it — clearing the list also clears the iCloud copy.
  • We have no user accounts or databases tied to your identity, so there is nothing for us to delete on your behalf.

Children's privacy

The app is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect any personal information from them.

Third parties

  • Cornell Lab of Ornithology / eBird: receives the search coordinates (rounded to roughly 1 km by our proxy) or country code, and API request metadata, relayed through our proxy when you search for sightings. See eBird's API Terms of Use.
  • iNaturalist (California Academy of Sciences / National Geographic Society): provides photo information and serves bird photos directly to your device.
  • Unsplash: serves the generic bird photo shown when a species has no suitably licensed photo.
  • Apple: MapKit maps, place-name lookup and place search; iCloud storage for life-list sync if you use iCloud.
  • Google Maps / Waze: only if you choose one of them for directions; they receive the sighting's coordinates.
  • Our backend proxy: a small server we operate solely to keep our eBird API credentials off your device. It forwards your search requests to eBird, briefly caches eBird's public responses as described above, and does not maintain user accounts or a database of your activity.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy in a way that affects how your data is handled, we will update this page and the "Last updated" date above. Material changes will also be surfaced in-app.
